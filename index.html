The Impact of AI-Generated Text on the Internet

Jonas Dolezal 1, Sawood Alam 2, Mark Graham 2, Maty Bohacek 3*

1 Imperial College London    2 Internet Archive    3 Stanford University

We construct a representative sample of websites published on the internet between 2022 and 2025 using the Internet Archive, and apply a state-of-the-art AI text detector. By mid-2025, roughly 35% of newly published websites were classified as AI-generated or AI-assisted, up from zero before ChatGPT's launch in late 2022. We find statistically significant evidence that increases in AI-generated text correlate negatively with semantic diversity and positively with the prevalence of positive sentiment, but we do not find evidence for decreased factual accuracy or stylistic diversity. This diverges from public perception: in a survey of US adults, the majority believed in all of these negative impacts.

Key Findings
  • ~35% of new websites are AI-generated or AI-assisted by mid-2025, up from effectively zero before ChatGPT's launch in November 2022.
  • We find evidence that the rise of AI-generated text on the internet brings about a semantic contraction (reduced diversity of topics) and a positivity shift (increased positive sentiment).
  • The majority of US adults believe in all six tested negative impacts of AI-generated text on the internet, while we only confirm two.
  • Beliefs correlate with AI usage and favorability: infrequent AI users and AI skeptics are significantly more likely to believe in negative impacts of AI-generated text on the internet.

Hypothesis Results & Participant Study

For each hypothesis, we show (a) the correlation between the measured signal and AI prevalence across monthly samples, (b) the overall distribution of participant survey responses, (c) responses broken down by AI usage frequency, and (d) responses by general view of AI's impact on society.

Hypothesis 1: Semantic Contraction Confirmed • ρ = 0.47, p = 0.004

"As AI text becomes more common on the internet, the range of unique ideas and diverse viewpoints shrinks."

(a) Quantitative Analysis. Left: Average pairwise cosine similarity of semantic embeddings plotted against AI Likelihood score (ρ = 0.47, p = 0.004). Right: Both signals over time, showing parallel trends that confirm the hypothesis. AI-generated websites had 33% higher semantic similarity than non-AI websites (0.0701 vs. 0.0526).

(b) Overall survey responses. 60.9% lean towards agreement.
